# Examples

All sample text is made up for illustration. Each pair lists the rules it fixes.

## Slop to fix

**1. NS-P-02, NS-P-20**
Before: It's worth noting that, in today's fast-paced world, our new export feature is available.
After: The export feature is now available.

**2. NS-P-04**
Before: It's not just a dashboard, it's a command center for your team.
After: The dashboard shows each person's open tasks and what is blocked.

**3. NS-P-05**
Before: Not a template. Not a framework. A way of working.
After: A short checklist the team runs before every release.

**4. NS-P-06**
Before: The result: faster onboarding. Why? Because the setup is automated.
After: Onboarding is faster because the setup runs by itself.

**5. NS-P-08**
Before: The data tells us that the onboarding flow is losing people, and the problem becomes a priority.
After: Dana's team saw that new users stop at step 3 and made it the first fix. (Add the count only if the source has it.)

**6. NS-P-09**
Before: Nobody really designed the permission model. It just grew.
After: We added permissions one request at a time and never stepped back to design them.

**7. NS-P-10, NS-P-11**
Before: ...so we moved the deadline to 14 May. In the end, it's about people, not process. And that changes everything.
After: ...so we moved the deadline to 14 May.

**8. NS-P-12**
Before: The tool imports files. The platform then validates them. Finally the solution stores the results.
After: The tool imports files, validates them and stores the results.

**9. NS-P-16, NS-P-17**
Before: We leverage cutting-edge AI to deliver a seamless, best-in-class experience that plays a pivotal role in your growth.
After: The assistant drafts replies from your past tickets. You approve each one before it sends.

**10. NS-P-18**
Before: Studies show that most teams skip retrospectives.
After: In our last survey of 12 teams, 7 skipped them. (Use only if the survey exists; else cut the sentence.)

**11. NS-P-19**
Before: It could perhaps be argued that this approach may potentially reduce costs to some extent.
After: This approach may cut costs. We have not measured it.

**12. NS-P-22**
Before: We are committed to delivering excellence to every customer.
After: We reply to support tickets within four working hours. (Use your real number or cut the line.)

**13. NS-P-13**
Before: "## 🚀 **Key Takeaways**" followed by three bullets that are each one full sentence of a single argument.
After: One paragraph that states the argument in order, no header.

**14. NS-P-25**
Before: Great question! I hope this helps. Feel free to reach out if you have any more questions.
After: (delete all three; start at the answer)

**15. NS-P-14, NS-P-15**
Before: Moreover, the tool is fast. Furthermore, the tool is simple. Additionally, the tool is reliable.
After: It is fast and simple. In a month of use it has not crashed.  (second fact only if true)

## Voice to keep (do not flatten)

**V1. Blunt, short, a little rude. Keep.**
Draft: Honestly the old billing page was garbage. Nobody could find the invoice button. We fixed it. Moved the button. Done.
Right edit: no change, or at most "Honestly" kept. The fragments and the bluntness are the author's. Rewriting this as one smooth sentence would remove the person.
Wrong edit: "The previous billing page had usability issues, which we resolved by relocating the invoice button."

**V2. Spoken cadence with a real hedge. Keep.**
Draft: I think, though I'm not sure, that the bug is in the retry loop. Could be the proxy. I'll know by Friday.
Right edit: no change. The hedge marks real doubt (NS-P-19 does not apply), and "I'll know by Friday" is a commitment.
Wrong edit: "The bug is likely in the retry loop or proxy; confirmation expected Friday."

**V3. Signature repetition and a wry closer. Keep.**
Draft: It was slow. It was so slow that Linh made tea, drank the tea, and came back to find it still going. That's the whole story, really.
Right edit: no change. The repeated "slow" and the tea are specific and funny; the closing line is the author's tone, not a fake-profound kicker (see `voice.md`, "when a rule may break"). If you cut anything, cut nothing.
Wrong edit: "The process was extremely slow, taking several minutes."
