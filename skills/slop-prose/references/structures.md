# Structures

Patterns at sentence and paragraph level. Each entry: what it looks like, why it fails, the fix.

## Binary contrast (NS-P-04)
"It's not X, it's Y." Knocks down a claim nobody made, then props up the real one. Fix: state Y. Allowed when X is a view someone holds and the text answers it.

## Negative listing (NS-P-05)
"Not a plugin. Not a framework. A habit." Fix: say what it is, in one sentence.

## Colon reveal and rhetorical setup (NS-P-06)
"The result: faster builds." / "What does this mean? Simple." A drumroll before a small point. Fix: put the point in the sentence. Self-answered questions stay only when a reader would really ask them.

## Dramatic fragments (NS-P-07)
"Speed. Focus. Results." Stacked for effect. Fix: one real sentence. One fragment for a real change of pace is fine.

## False agency (NS-P-08)
"The data tells us", "the market decided", "the complaint becomes a fix". Things do not act for people. Fix: name who did it. "Maria's team read the complaint and shipped a fix."

## Narrator from a distance (NS-P-09)
"Nobody planned this." "Teams often find that." The writer floats above the scene. Fix: a named person, "you", or the writer's own "I".

## Fake-profound kicker (NS-P-10)
A closing line built to be quoted: "And that changes everything." Fix: delete. End on the last concrete fact or the next action. Do not swap it for a fresher metaphor.

## Recap ending (NS-P-11)
A last paragraph that repeats the body. Fix: cut it, or end on the decision, date or next step.

## Synonym cycling (NS-P-12)
"The tool... the platform... the solution... the product" for one thing. Fix: pick the plain noun and repeat it. Repetition is clearer than variety.

## Formatting slop (NS-P-13)
Emoji in headings, bold on random phrases, bullets for a sentence that wants to flow, a header over two lines, a table with one row. Fix: prose where the logic is connected; lists only for parallel items.

## Sentence starters (NS-P-14)
Wh- openers ("What matters is..."), a chain of "However", three sentences starting with the same word. Fix: lead with the subject; connect ideas with real logic or nothing.

## Rhythm (NS-P-15)
Equal-length sentences, a rule of three in every paragraph, each paragraph ending on a punchy short line. Fix: vary length; two items often beat three; end paragraphs differently. Do not engineer rhythm into a text that already has one.

## Em dash (NS-P-21)
A tell when used in every other sentence. None in short text, at most two in a long piece. Replace with a comma, colon, period or parentheses.

## Portability (NS-P-22)
A generic declarative that fits any subject: "The reasons are structural." Fix: name the reasons.
